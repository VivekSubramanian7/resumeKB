# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Anyone building professional self-knowledge: a developer reflecting after a deep sprint, a job-seeker crystallizing their narrative before interviews, a knowledge worker capturing insights as they happen. The common thread is someone who wants to understand what they know and have done, not just store it. They come to this in quiet moments and busy ones alike.

## Product Purpose

A second brain that doesn't just receive — it **probes**. resumeKB captures professional experience through voice, text, and document ingestion, then uses structured extraction to build a living knowledge graph. The planned dialogue mode flips the relationship: the app asks thought-provoking questions to draw out knowledge the user didn't know they had. Success is when a user discovers connections in their own experience they'd never have articulated unprompted.

## Positioning

The professional memory that thinks back at you.

## Brand Personality

**Contemplative, alive, intimate.** A quiet space that breathes — present, responsive, personal. Not a productivity tool barking at you. Not a passive database waiting. Something in between: an entity that listens, then asks the question you needed to hear.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboards** — white cards on gray, blue accent, sidebar nav, metric widgets. The Stripe/Linear clone aesthetic.
- **Notion clones** — minimal to the point of boring, blocks on white, no personality, no atmosphere.
- **Gamified tools** — streaks, badges, confetti, cartoon illustrations. Duolingo energy applied to professional tools.
- **Corporate enterprise** — dense, gray, Excel-adjacent, designed by committee for compliance.

## Design Principles

1. **Breathe, don't shout.** Every screen should feel like a space you want to be in, not a task you have to complete. Rhythm over density.
2. **Alive, not animated.** The interface should feel responsive and present — subtle shifts that signal awareness — not choreographed transitions demanding attention.
3. **Show the mind at work.** When the app processes, extracts, or connects ideas, make that visible. The intelligence isn't hidden infrastructure; it's the product's personality.
4. **Intimate scale.** This is a personal tool. Design for one person's world, not a team's workspace. Every element should feel like it belongs to you.
5. **Depth over decoration.** Visual richness comes from layered surfaces, atmospheric light, and spatial relationships — not from ornament or illustration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard best practices: sufficient contrast, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion alternatives for all animation. No specific WCAG level mandated, but don't sacrifice usability for atmosphere.
